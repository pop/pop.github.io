Phil Spector (obligatory "problematic" acknowledgement) coined the term "Wall of Sound". This was a process of producing music which the ultimate effect was to overwhelm the listener with sound in a way they had never heard before. This produced some great hits and influenced music from the 60s onward.

Speed Racer is a Wall of Light

That's the only way I can describe it.

Every frame is of course a painting in that there is a lot going on and story telling is happening in multiple levels, but the way the movie plays with colors, camera work, and a mix of 3D, green screen, and 2D elements is unrivaled.

I'm not the only person to say this, there has been a reevaluation of Speed Racer recently on YouTube and I welcome it. Especially because I have a 3 year old who loves anything with an engine and wheels and this is his new favorite movie!

I want to point to Spy Kids as similar in production value, in that it targets a similar age range and has a similar green-screen heavy production with lots of cartooney 3d assets and environments... but Speed Racer is SO MUCH MORE.

To illustrate there is a scene with two people racing on these Mario Kart-esque race courses and instead of just cutting between them as they talk over a mic, the camera zooms from one face to another over the course of the conversation. Of course they were filmed separately and the camera work is entirely virtual but still!

Some movies, like Marvel films recently, use CGI to lower production costs without doing anything that groundbreaking. She Hulk was a very expensive CGI model to achieve... basically a green painted Tatiana Maslany (which isn't to say the show is bad, nor that the CGI is bad, just that the direction is backward when you spend so much money on a talking head).

Speed Racer on the other hand is paying for 12 bits of color and 1 million polygons and by god they're gonna get their money's worth.

This movie is unlike anything I have ever seen before and probably will ever see in the future, unless they magically get money to do a sequel... and I hope they do.
