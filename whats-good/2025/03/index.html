Film Photography

In February I picked up a film camera that Lucy inherited from her grandpa. I took some amazing pictures of the snowfall we got, my toddler sledding, I got really into it! I finished a roll, wound back the film, opened the camera and... it was empty! Doh! I didn't even think to check if it was loaded!

I recovered from this devastating self-prank, loaded the camera, and shot a roll.

The inherited camera was... kinda jank.

Don't get me wrong, it's an aesthetic... but I also only got like 3-4 useful pictures out of a roll of 37 which is a bad ratio.

Most of the picutres were not this cool dream vibe -- they were just blown our or totally blank.

As I got more into it, I acquired two cameras that worked more reliably:

  1. A cheap Ilford Reusable Disposable camera.
  2. Lucy got me a refurbished Olympus OM-1!

The "Reusable Disposable" sounds like an oxymoron but it's exactly what you'd expect:

  • Basic plastic shell
  • Static lens
  • Static exposure settings
  • Flash
  • Rewind so you can load and unload your own film

I shot my first successful roll of Color film on the Illford and it was great!

The OM-1 is a really nice manual film camera and lens -- a "camera system". Lucy got it refurbished for ~$60 which feels like a steal given the build quality!

I love this camera.

I've also been developing the film at home which for me has been half the fun!

  • For black and white film I do "Stand Development" where you dilute your developer 1:100 so the process takes ~1 hour but you can't over-develop.
  • For color film development I'm just doing C41 development, which required me to buy a Sous Vide!

Yay kitchen sink science!

I have zero interest in film printing so I've recruited Lucy to digitize the photos by taking pictures with her DSLR (also an Olympus camera -- we're an Olympus family I guess) and inverting + editing them in Gimp. The results are great!
