I built my first gaming PC with my dad when I was 13 in ~2009.

Before that I was using a bunch of really low powered laptops and desktops that didn't have dedicated graphics and were certified for Windows 98 so you know... they barely connected to the internet.

Building a PC really is just like complex legos, but much like building Ikea furniture, the fact that I had a hand in building this machine made me way more invested in it's operation and empowered me to fix, tinker, and upgrade it over the years.

On top of building though I also sort of designed the machine, choosing the parts, double checking that everything would fit together and was compatible, and finding a good deal for everything, forced me to have a deeper knowledge of the machine.

It was fun to assemble this complex device and then to be able to play any game I could want on the pretty budget hardware, around $1,000 all told at the time.

Over the years I realized that Windows kinda sucked actually and I should ditch it.
Thankfully, Valve has been building up an entire ecosystem of software to play Windows games on Linux so well that it Just Works (TM) -- something I never would have said about _anything_ Linux related when I was 13.
