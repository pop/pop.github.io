> TLDR: if your devices are set to limit charging, you can "over-charge" to 100% and it feels like a free upgrade.

For over a year now I've set all of my devices to restrict charging to 70%-90% of their maximum capacity.

Of course I did this because we've all heard that it helps improve battery health.
Most of my devices are plugged in _most_ of the time.
* My laptops are basically desktops, charging all day and rarely un-plugged.
* My Steam Deck is docked most of the time as well, I don't play video games _that_ much (I wish).
* My phone is plugged in the least, but that's still charging ~8-10 hours a day.

I can't say anecdotally if this is improving the battery health of my devices, but I haven't noticed any issues.
At least with the lifestyle I live, less than 100% battery is fine.
Sure I usually end my day at ~15% battery on my phone, but that's still a few _hours_ of use right before bed.

Assuming this _actually_ works, I think we should consider making something like this the default.
Battery life is one of those things that you just get used to.
At least I have learned to intuit, ok if I watch a movie I'll need to plug in sooner; if I get a new phone I'll still hit the charge limits at some point and I'll just need to learn where those are.

Here's the secret: you can do ad-hoc over-charges.

This framing is actually really nice.
If I know I'm traveling today and I won't be able to charge reliably, I will charge my devices to 100% just that once.
Then it feels like I'm _lousy_ with battery life.
If my device is usually charged to 70% and I charge it to 100% that's almost increasing my battery by 50%!
Can you imagine if you could just increase your battery by 50% whenever you wanted?
Most of the time you don't need it, but when you do it's _such_ a luxury.
